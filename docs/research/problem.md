# Sealed — Problem research (Phase 0)

Researched 2026-10-17. **Launch scenario: buying a car from dealers.** Moving was the first pick; we switched because car quotes can be compared like-for-like (same model, same trim, out-the-door price), which makes "compete using only true facts" easy to show and easy to defend.

Labels: **DOCUMENTED** (a source says it; link given), **CLAIMED** (a company says it about itself), **ASSUMED** (our judgment, not checked). Nothing here is VERIFIED; it is all web research. Quotes from people are kept short; follow the link for the full post.

## 1. Evidence of the pain

### 1.1 Car buying (launch scenario)

| What | Evidence | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Most buyers don't make dealers compete | In 2019, 41% of buyers visited just one dealership (up from 30% in 2017); the average was 2.3 dealers. [Cox Automotive](https://www.coxautoinc.com/insights-hub/car-buyers-visiting-fewer-dealerships-making-faster-decisions-as-online-engagement-rises/) | DOCUMENTED |
| It takes a long time | Average total shopping time: 13 hours 55 minutes. (same Cox link) | DOCUMENTED |
| Few negotiate online | Less than 10% of new-vehicle buyers negotiated their final price online; those who did were notably more satisfied. (same Cox link) | DOCUMENTED |
| The process is frustrating | 55% of new-car and 57% of used-car buyers felt frustrated during the purchase, mainly because of the time it took (IHS, 2014). [Cox Automotive](https://www.coxautoinc.com/insights-hub/3-car-buyer-frustrations-digital-retailing-can-solve/) | DOCUMENTED (old data) |
| Competition saves money | Using an online referral service lowered the price paid by about 2.2% after controlling for who uses it. [NBER w8667](https://www.nber.org/papers/w8667.pdf). An earlier paper: online buyers did better than 61% of offline buyers. [NBER w7961](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7961) | DOCUMENTED (California, ~2000 data) |
| Asking works | CR survey of 1,006 used-car buyers: 70% haggled, 83% of them got a better deal, median saving $900 (8% off asking). Most successful negotiators just asked for a better price. [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/used-car-buying/why-haggling-for-your-next-car-really-pays) | DOCUMENTED |
| The playbook is public | Email several dealers for an out-the-door price and ask each to beat the best one: [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/car-pricing-negotiation/how-to-negotiate-car-prices-by-email-or-phone), [AutoTempest](https://blog.autotempest.com/?p=880), [CarEdge](https://caredge.com/guides/get-car-dealers-to-compete-for-your-business), NerdWallet via [CSMonitor](https://csmonitor.com/Business/Saving-Money/2016/0713/A-faster-way-to-get-a-quote-on-your-dream-car) | DOCUMENTED |
| Dealers make it awkward | Dealer forum: the internet manager only "answer[s] the initial request" and hands the lead to a salesperson. [Edmunds forum](https://forums.edmunds.com/discussion/comment/1704005). CR warns that "special internet prices" and confusing listings are common. (CR link above) | DOCUMENTED |
| Weekend constraint | Pennsylvania bans Sunday car sales; dealers there are closed Sundays. About 13 states ban or restrict them. [CPBJ](https://www.cpbj.com/for-car-dealers-sunday-sales-remain-no-go/), [Jalopnik](https://www.jalopnik.com/2210294/bans-blue-laws-13-states-sunday-car-sales/) | DOCUMENTED |

**The key pattern:** competing quotes save money and the method is public, yet 41% of buyers talk to just one dealer and fewer than 10% negotiate online. The gap is time and awkwardness, not knowledge. That is what Sealed fills.

## 3. Persona (fictional)

**Arjun, 24**, finishing a master's in December and starting a job in January that he'll drive to. He has picked the car: one specific new compact sedan, one trim, one color (exact model to be fixed before the demo). He just needs to buy it at a fair price before January.

Why he hates doing it:
- Every dealer wants him to "come in today"; Saturdays disappear.
- Quotes arrive in different shapes (MSRP, "internet price", payments per month) with add-ons and fees buried.
- He knows he *should* make dealers compete but finds it awkward, so he'll probably talk to one dealer, like 41% of buyers do.
- He doesn't want any dealer to learn the most he's willing to pay.

## 4. Problem statement, story, why now, objections

### Problem statement

> Getting dealers to compete lowers the price — about 2% in NBER's study, and 83% of used-car buyers who asked got a better deal — yet 41% of buyers visit just one dealership, because chasing several sellers is slow and awkward.

### Before / after (illustrative; no real prices)

**Without Sealed.** Arjun spends Saturday at one dealer. He gets a monthly payment, not a total. A second dealer's email quote leaves out fees. He doesn't want to haggle, so he signs at the first dealer. He never finds out what the others would have charged.

**With Sealed.** He enters the exact car and his zip code once. Fo, openly as his AI assistant, asks five dealers for a written out-the-door price for that exact car, by email, and calls the two that don't reply. Sealed only counts a quote if it's an out-the-door total for the right trim; vague replies get one follow-up asking for the total. Then each dealer gets one honest message: "Your out-the-door price is $X; the lowest written out-the-door quote so far is $Y. Would you like to revise?" Arjun sees comparable totals side by side, picks one, approves it, and the others get a polite "thanks, he went another way." His maximum price never left his screen.

### Why now

- Fo has its own email, phone number and voice, and calls businesses that have no API. [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/vinod-khosla-backs-wajo-fo-ai-agent/), [Progressive Robot](https://www.progressiverobot.com/2026/09/29/wajo-launches-fo-ai-agent-calls-payments/) — DOCUMENTED
- Wajo's founder describes this exact job: call five plumbers, size them up, negotiate, book the best. [wajo.ai](https://wajo.ai/blog/unveiling-wajo/) — CLAIMED
- Referral services did this 20 years ago, but they routed buyers to one or two paying dealers ([NBER w7961](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7961)). An agent with its own inbox and phone can reach any dealer, on the buyer's side. — ASSUMED (our framing)

### Top 3 objections

1. **"Dealers ignore AI emails."**
   Untested. Dealers have internet sales staff whose job is to answer email leads (CR, NerdWallet links above). Fo sends openly as an AI assistant (VERIFIED in our spikes). Fallback: Fo calls non-responders. The real-world round this weekend is the test (section 5).

2. **"Isn't this manipulative?"**
   Sealed only states true facts: real written competing quotes, the real car, the buyer's real timeline. No invented quotes, no fake deadlines, no pretending to be the buyer. Telling a dealer the best competing price is what Consumer Reports and CarEdge recommend. Dealers who lose get a polite decline. Wajo's own trust paper counts unapproved sends and overshared private details as failures ([wajo.ai](https://wajo.ai/blog/trust-and-task-completion/)); Sealed uses the same standard.
   Test: run the outbound audit over every message in the demo; show zero ceiling mentions and zero unverifiable claims.

3. **"I could do it myself."**
   True, and the method is public. But 41% of buyers talk to one dealer, fewer than 10% negotiate online, and shopping takes about 14 hours (Cox). The problem isn't knowing how; it's doing it.

## 5. Demo plan: real world first, stand-ins as fallback

### Option A — real dealers (preferred)

Only honest if the purchase intent is real. Before any send:

- **Real buyer.** Someone on the team (or a friend who agrees) is genuinely shopping for this exact car. If nobody is, use Option B. Asking dealers for quotes with no intent to buy would break Sealed's own "true facts only" rule.
- **Every message must be true.** State only the real timeline ("planning to buy within N weeks" only if true). Never "ready to buy today" unless it is.
- **The user or a team member approves each send** (project rule), and Fo will also ask for confirmation before emailing anyone (DOCUMENTED in the Fo spec).
- **No ceiling, ever**, in any message, to Fo or to dealers.
- **Pick dealers that work weekends.** PA dealers are closed Sunday (DOCUMENTED). Choose dealers in a state without a Sunday ban (ASSUMED: check each dealer's hours), and send **today (Saturday)** so replies can come Saturday evening or Sunday.
- **Decline unused dealers politely** at the end, even in a demo.
- Timeline risk: replies may not arrive before Sunday evening. Decide by **Sunday noon** whether to switch to Option B.

### Option B — stand-in dealers (fallback)

- 3 consenting people with real inboxes (one is rfb5381@psu.edu) play dealers, replying with scripted but realistic answers:
  - Dealer 1: a clean written out-the-door total.
  - Dealer 2: an MSRP plus "fees extra" (not comparable → Sealed asks for the total).
  - Dealer 3: replies late, then revises down after round 2.
- Label it on screen as a staged round with stand-in dealers.

### What success looks like on screen (60 seconds)

1. One form: exact car, zip code, timeline. Fo sends requests (show the outbound text: same spec, open AI disclosure, no budget).
2. Replies arrive as quote cards; the "fees extra" one is flagged "not out-the-door" and gets a follow-up.
3. Round 2: each dealer gets one true message with the best written competing total. One revises down.
4. Arjun approves one; the others are declined politely.
5. Counter: "N messages sent · 0 mentions of your maximum · 0 unverified claims."

Phone path: one Fo call to a non-responding dealer (or a stand-in) if outbound calling works through a text request. Test with a call to our own phone first; whether a transcript comes back through MCP is UNTESTED.
